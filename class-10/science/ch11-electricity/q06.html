11.6 A copper wire has diameter 0.5 mm and resistivity of 1.6 × 10–8 Ω m. What will be the length of this wire to make its resistance 10 Ω? How much does the resistance change if the diameter is doubled?

Given

  • d = 0.5 mm; ρ = 1.6 × 10−8 Ω m; R = 10 Ω.

Asked

The length; the effect of doubling the diameter.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a direct calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. A = π(0.25 × 10−3)2 ≈ 1.96 × 10−7 m2.
  2. l = RA/ρ = 10 × 1.96 × 10−7/1.6 × 10−8 ≈ 122.7 m.
  3. Doubling the diameter makes the area 4 times, so R becomes one-fourth: 2.5 Ω (it falls by 7.5 Ω).
