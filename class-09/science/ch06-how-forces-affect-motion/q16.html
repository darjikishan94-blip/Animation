Exercise 6.16 · Why the compass needle moves, not the magnet

6.16 When the pole of a bar magnet is brought close to a magnetic compass, the bar magnet and the compass needle (which is also a magnet) exert a magnetic force on each other. As per Newton’s third law of motion, both the forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. However, the compass needle moves, whereas the bar magnet does not move (Fig. 6.42). Explain why.

Given

  • Equal and opposite magnetic forces.

Asked

Why only the needle moves.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for a short explanation; the page shows the question and the answer.

Answer

  1. The forces are equal, but they act on different bodies with different conditions.
  2. The needle is very light and pivoted almost without friction, so even a small force turns it (large acceleration, a = F/m). The bar magnet is much heavier and is held in the hand (or rests on a surface), so the same small force is balanced by the hand or friction and it does not move noticeably.

The light, freely pivoted needle responds; the heavy, held magnet does not.
