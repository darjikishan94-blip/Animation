Q1 State whether True or False. (i) Work is said to be done when a force is applied, even if the object does not move. (ii) Lifting a bucket vertically upward results in positive work done on the bucket. (iii) The SI unit for both work and energy is joule (J). (iv) A motionless stretched rubber band has kinetic energy. (v) Energy can change from one form to another.

Given

  • Five statements about work and energy.

Asked

Whether each statement is true or false.

How the animation is drawn

  • Each statement gets a short scene that tests it. The scenes are illustrative and use no numbers.
  • The TRUE / FALSE labels and the reasons appear only when the answers are shown.
  • The box slides on a smooth floor and the pendulum swings without air resistance, so no energy is lost in either.

Answer

  1. (i) False. Work = force × displacement. If the object does not move, the displacement is zero, so no work is done, however hard you push.
  2. (ii) True. The lifting force and the displacement are both upward (angle 0°), so the work done on the bucket is positive.
  3. (iii) True. 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre. Work done on a body equals the energy transferred to it, so work and energy share the same unit.
  4. (iv) False. It is not moving, so its kinetic energy is zero. Because it is stretched, it stores elastic potential energy.
  5. (v) True. For example, in a swinging pendulum potential energy and kinetic energy keep changing into each other.

True: (ii), (iii), (v). False: (i), (iv).

Q2 Fill in the blanks. (i) Work done = ______ × ______ (in the direction of force). (ii) 1 joule of work is done when a force of ______ newton displaces an object by 1 metre in the direction of the force. (iii) The expression for kinetic energy of a body of mass m and velocity v is ______. (iv) The potential energy of an object of mass m at a small height h from the Earth’s surface is ______. (v) Power is defined as the ______ at which work is done.

Given

  • Five sentences, each with a blank.

Asked

The word or expression that completes each sentence.

How the animation is drawn

  • Each sentence gets a short scene. The scenes are illustrative.
  • The missing words appear only when the answers are shown.
  • In (v) the two loads and the heights are identical; only the time taken differs.

Answer

  1. (i) Work done = force × displacement (in the direction of force).
  2. (ii) 1 joule of work is done when a force of 1 newton displaces an object by 1 metre in the direction of the force.
  3. (iii) Kinetic energy = ½mv2.
  4. (iv) Potential energy = mgh.
  5. (v) Power is defined as the rate at which work is done.

force, displacement; 1; ½mv²; mgh; rate.

Q3 When a ball thrown upwards reaches its highest point, tick which of the following statement(s) are correct? (i) The force acting on the ball is zero. (ii) The acceleration of the ball is zero. (iii) Its kinetic energy is zero. (iv) Its potential energy is maximum.

Given

  • A ball is thrown straight upward.
  • It reaches its highest point and comes back down.

Asked

Which of the four statements are correct at the highest point.

How the animation is drawn

  • No speeds or heights are given, so the speed, height and timing are illustrative. What matters is the direction and relative size of each arrow.
  • Air resistance is neglected, so the only force is the weight mg and the acceleration is g, downward, throughout.
  • The velocity arrow’s length is proportional to the speed. The weight and acceleration arrows never change.
  • Faint circles show the ball at equal time gaps; the flight runs at half speed. The two energy bars share one scale.

Answer

  1. (i) Incorrect. The weight mg acts at every point of the flight, including the top.
  2. (ii) Incorrect. Acceleration = force ÷ mass = mg/m = g, downward, even at the top.
  3. (iii) Correct. The ball is at rest for an instant, v = 0, so KE = ½mv2 = 0.
  4. (iv) Correct. Its height is greatest there, so PE = mgh is maximum: all the kinetic energy it was thrown with has become potential energy.

Correct statements: (iii) and (iv). At the top only the velocity is zero; the force and the acceleration are not.

Q4 For each of the following situations, identify the energy transformation that takes place: (i) a truck moving uphill, (ii) unwinding of a watch spring, (iii) photosynthesis in green leaves, (iv) water flowing from a dam, (v) burning of a matchstick, (vi) explosion of a fire cracker, (vii) speaking into a microphone, (viii) a glowing electric bulb, and (ix) a solar panel.

Given

  • Nine everyday situations.

Asked

The energy transformation that takes place in each situation.

How the animation is drawn

  • Each situation gets a short scene. The drawings are simple and illustrative.
  • The energy boxes show “?” until the answers are shown.
  • Nearly every real change also gives out a little heat; it is listed only where it is a main product.

Answer

  1. (i) Chemical energy of the fuel → kinetic energy + gravitational potential energy (plus heat and sound).
  2. (ii) Elastic potential energy of the wound spring → kinetic energy of the moving parts.
  3. (iii) Light energy → chemical energy stored in food.
  4. (iv) Gravitational potential energy of the water → kinetic energy (which can then be turned into electrical energy).
  5. (v) Chemical energy → heat + light.
  6. (vi) Chemical energy → heat + light + sound + kinetic energy.
  7. (vii) Sound energy → electrical energy.
  8. (viii) Electrical energy → light + heat.
  9. (ix) Light (solar) energy → electrical energy.

In every case energy is not created or destroyed; it only changes from one form to another.
