Q8 A man (60 kg) rides a scooter (100 kg) up to speed v. The next day his son (40 kg) rides along, and the scooter reaches the same speed in the same time. How does the fuel used on the two days compare?

Scroll sideways inside the picture, or turn your phone sideways for a bigger view.

Given

  • Man 60 kg, scooter 100 kg. On Day 2 the son (40 kg) rides as well.
  • Same final speed v on both days, reached in the same time.
  • All the energy comes from the fuel. No losses to friction or air resistance.

Asked

The ratio of fuel used on Day 1 to fuel used on Day 2.

How the animation is drawn

  • Both rides start from rest and speed up evenly. The question does not say how the speed builds up; the fuel used depends only on the kinetic energy at the end, so this choice does not change the answer.
  • Velocity arrows are equal in both lanes at every instant. The driving-force arrows and the fuel bars use one scale for both days.
  • Dots under each road mark the scooter's position at equal time gaps. The gaps grow because the speed is increasing.
  • Scooter, man and son move together as one mass (wheel spin ignored).

Answer

  1. Total mass that speeds up: Day 1 = 60 + 100 = 160 kg. Day 2 = 60 + 100 + 40 = 200 kg.
  2. Starting from rest with nothing lost, fuel energy used = kinetic energy gained = ½mv2.
  3. Day 1: ½ × 160 × v2 = 80v2. Day 2: ½ × 200 × v2 = 100v2.
  4. The time t does not change the answer. Only the final speed matters, and it is the same on both days.

Fuel used, Day 1 : Day 2 = 80v2 : 100v2 = 4 : 5. Day 2 uses 25% more fuel.

Q9 A child sits on one side of a seesaw with sliding seats and an adult who weighs twice as much sits on the other. The seesaw is balanced. Draw the situation, showing how far from the fulcrum each one sits.

Scroll sideways inside the picture, or turn your phone sideways for a bigger view.

Given

  • A seesaw whose seats can slide along it.
  • The adult weighs twice as much as the child (2W and W).
  • The seesaw is balanced.

Asked

A figure of the balanced seesaw showing how far from the fulcrum the child and the adult sit.

How the animation is drawn

  • The plank is uniform and pivoted at its middle, so its own weight has no turning effect.
  • Weight arrows are to scale (the adult's is twice the child's), and so are the turning-effect bars.
  • Ticks on the plank are a quarter of d apart.
  • No masses are given, so how fast the seesaw tips in step 2 is only illustrative. Its direction is what matters.

Answer

  1. For balance, the anticlockwise turning effect equals the clockwise turning effect.
  2. Child: W × d. Adult at distance x from the fulcrum: 2W × x.
  3. 2W × x = W × d, so x = d/2.

The adult sits at half the child’s distance from the fulcrum: child at d, adult at d/2 (distances in the ratio 2 : 1). The heavier person sits closer to the fulcrum.

Q10 A 2 kg ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. (i) What is the sign of the work done by gravity on the way up and on the way down? (ii) With air resistance it rises only to 19.4 m. How much work did air resistance do? Take g = 10 m/s².

Scroll sideways inside the picture, or turn your phone sideways for a bigger view.

Given

  • Mass of the ball 2 kg, thrown straight up at 20 m/s.
  • g = 10 m/s².
  • With air resistance acting, the ball rises to 19.4 m.

Asked

(i) The sign of the work done by gravity on the way up and on the way down. (ii) The work done by air resistance as the ball rises.

How the animation is drawn

  • The height scale is true to size. Heights are measured from the launch point.
  • Air resistance always opposes the motion. Here it has a constant size, set so the ball tops out at 19.4 m. The question does not describe it on the way down, so the same size is used there.
  • The air-resistance arrow is enlarged so it can be seen. The real force is much smaller than mg.
  • Flight runs at half speed, and faint circles show the ball every 0.2 s. The energy bar's parts share one scale.

Answer

  1. (i) Going up, gravity (down) is opposite to the displacement (up). The angle is 180°, so W = mgh cos 180° = −mgh. Coming down they are in the same direction. The angle is 0°, so W = +mgh.
  2. Over 19.4 m, mgh = 2 × 10 × 19.4 = 388 J. Gravity does −388 J going up and +388 J coming back down to the launch level.
  3. (ii) Kinetic energy at launch = ½ × 2 × 202 = 400 J. At the highest point KE = 0 and PE = 388 J.
  4. Work–energy theorem for the rise: Wgravity + Wair = KEtop − KElaunch, so −388 + Wair = 0 − 400, giving Wair = −12 J.
  5. Check: without air resistance the ball would rise to u2/2g = 400/20 = 20 m, a little higher than 19.4 m.

(i) The work done by gravity is negative on the way up and positive on the way down. (ii) The work done by air resistance is −12 J: air resistance takes 12 J of the ball’s energy.
