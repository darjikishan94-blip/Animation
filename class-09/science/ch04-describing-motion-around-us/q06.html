4.6 Fig. 4.27 shows a position-time graph of two objects A and B that are moving along the parallel tracks in the same direction. Do objects A and B ever have equal velocity? Justify your answer.

Given

  • Fig. 4.27: two straight position–time lines; B starts ahead, A has the steeper line; they cross at t = 5 s.

Asked

Do they ever have equal velocity?

How the animation is drawn

  • Lines drawn to match Fig. 4.27 (positions not labelled in the figure).

Answer

  1. Velocity = slope of the position–time graph.
  2. Both lines are straight, so each velocity is constant; A’s line is steeper, so A is always faster.
  3. They never have equal velocity. At t = 5 s they are at the same position (the lines cross), not moving at the same velocity.

No: A’s slope (velocity) is always greater; they only meet at 5 s.
