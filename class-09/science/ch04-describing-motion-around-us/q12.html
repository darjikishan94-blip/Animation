4.12 The velocity-time graph from 0 s to 120 s for a cyclist is shown in Fig. 4.30. Shade the areas (in different colours) representing the displacement of the cyclist (i) while cyclist is moving with constant velocity. (ii) when the velocity of cyclist is decreasing. Also, calculate the displacement and average acceleration in the 120 s time interval.

Given

  • Fig. 4.30: v rises from 0 to 3 m/s (0–20 s), stays 3 m/s (20–100 s), falls to 2 m/s (100–120 s).

Asked

Shaded areas; displacement and average acceleration over 120 s.

How the animation is drawn

  • Graph drawn from the points of Fig. 4.30.

Answer

  1. (i) Constant velocity (20–100 s): area = 80 × 3 = 240 m.
  2. (ii) Decreasing velocity (100–120 s): area = ½(3 + 2) × 20 = 50 m.
  3. Plus 0–20 s: ½ × 20 × 3 = 30 m. Total displacement = 320 m.
  4. Average acceleration = (2 − 0)/120 = 1/60 ≈ 0.017 m s−2.

240 m and 50 m shaded; 320 m; ≈ 0.017 m s−2.
