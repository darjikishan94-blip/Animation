11.6 Two cylinders A and B of equal capacity are connected to each other via a stopcock. A contains a gas at standard temperature and pressure. B is completely evacuated. The entire system is thermally insulated. The stopcock is suddenly opened. Answer the following : (a) What is the final pressure of the gas in A and B ? (b) What is the change in internal energy of the gas ? (c) What is the change in the temperature of the gas ? (d) Do the intermediate states of the system (before settling to the final equilibrium state) lie on its P-V-T surface ?

Given

  • Equal volumes; A at STP, B evacuated; everything insulated; the stopcock is opened suddenly.

Asked

(a) Final pressure (b) ΔU (c) ΔT (d) whether the intermediate states are on the P–V–T surface.

How the animation is drawn

  • Molecules illustrative.

Answer

  1. (a) The volume doubles at the same temperature: P = 1 atm/2 = 0.5 atm (in both).
  2. (b) No heat (insulated) and no work (the gas pushes against vacuum): ΔU = 0.
  3. (c) For an ideal gas U depends only on T, so ΔT = 0.
  4. (d) No: the sudden (free) expansion is not quasi-static; while it rushes over, the gas has no single pressure and temperature, so those states are not equilibrium states on the P–V–T surface.

(a) 0.5 atm (b) 0 (c) 0 (d) No.
