13.2 Which of the following examples represent (nearly) simple harmonic motion and which represent periodic but not simple harmonic motion? (a) the rotation of earth about its axis. (b) motion of an oscillating mercury column in a U-tube. (c) motion of a ball bearing inside a smooth curved bowl, when released from a point slightly above the lower most point. (d) general vibrations of a polyatomic molecule about its equilibrium position.

Given

  • Four motions.

Asked

Which are (nearly) SHM, and which only periodic.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for a classification or a short derivation rather than describing one situation to follow; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. (a) Periodic, not SHM: a rotation, not a to-and-fro motion about a mean position.
  2. (b) SHM: the restoring force is proportional to the displacement of the column.
  3. (c) (Nearly) SHM, for small displacements.
  4. (d) Periodic but not SHM: a mixture of several SHMs of different frequencies.

SHM: (b), (c). Periodic only: (a), (d).
