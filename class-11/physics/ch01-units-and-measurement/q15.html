1.15 One mole of an ideal gas at standard temperature and pressure occupies 22.4 L (molar volume). What is the ratio of molar volume to the atomic volume of a mole of hydrogen ? (Take the size of hydrogen molecule to be about 1 Å). Why is this ratio so large ?

Given

  • Molar volume = 22.4 L = 22.4 × 10−3 m3; size of a hydrogen molecule ≈ 1 Å.

Asked

The ratio of molar volume to atomic volume, and why it is so large.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a calculation (or an explanation) with no physical situation to animate; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. Taking a molecule of size (diameter) 1 Å, radius 0.5 Å, the atomic volume of a mole ≈ 3.15 × 10−7 m3 (as in 1.14).
  2. Ratio = 22.4 × 10−3 / 3.15 × 10−7 ≈ 7.1 × 104.
  3. It is large because in a gas the molecules are far apart: the space between them is enormous compared with their own size.

About 7 × 104: gas molecules are widely separated.
