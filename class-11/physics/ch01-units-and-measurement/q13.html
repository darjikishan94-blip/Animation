1.13 A famous relation in physics relates ‘moving mass’ m to the ‘rest mass’ mo of a particle in terms of its speed v and the speed of light, c. (This relation first arose as a consequence of special relativity due to Albert Einstein). A boy recalls the relation almost correctly but forgets where to put the constant c. He writes : m = m0 / (1 − v2)1/2. Guess where to put the missing c.

Given

  • m = m0/(1 − v2)1/2, with c missing.

Asked

Where the speed of light c goes.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a calculation (or an explanation) with no physical situation to animate; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. m and m0 have the same dimensions, so (1 − v2)1/2 must be dimensionless.
  2. 1 is dimensionless, so v2 must be divided by a speed squared: v2/c2.

m = m0 / (1 − v2/c2)1/2
