1.8 Answer the following : (a) You are given a thread and a metre scale. How will you estimate the diameter of the thread ? (b) A screw gauge has a pitch of 1.0 mm and 200 divisions on the circular scale. Do you think it is possible to increase the accuracy of the screw gauge arbitrarily by increasing the number of divisions on the circular scale ? (c) The mean diameter of a thin brass rod is to be measured by vernier callipers. Why is a set of 100 measurements of the diameter expected to yield a more reliable estimate than a set of 5 measurements only ?

Given

  • (a) A thread and a metre scale. (b) Pitch 1.0 mm, 200 circular divisions. (c) 100 readings compared with 5.

Asked

How to find the thread’s diameter; whether more divisions give arbitrary accuracy; why 100 readings beat 5.

How the animation is drawn

  • (a) The number of turns is illustrative. (c) The readings are illustrative random values, not data.

Answer

  1. (a) Wind the thread on the scale (or a pencil) in n close turns with no gaps, measure the length L they cover; diameter = L/n.
  2. (b) The least count is pitch/divisions = 1.0/200 = 0.005 mm, and more divisions make it smaller in principle. But not arbitrarily: the divisions become too close to read, and backlash and an imperfect screw thread limit the accuracy.
  3. (c) Each reading carries a random error. Over many readings the errors tend to cancel, so the mean of 100 is much closer to the true value than the mean of 5 (the random error of the mean falls roughly as 1/√n).

(a) d = L/n. (b) No, not arbitrarily. (c) Random errors average out over more readings.
