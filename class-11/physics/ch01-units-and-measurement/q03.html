1.3 A calorie is a unit of heat (energy in transit) and it equals about 4.2 J where 1J = 1 kg m2 s–2. Suppose we employ a system of units in which the unit of mass equals α kg, the unit of length equals β m, the unit of time is γ s. Show that a calorie has a magnitude 4.2 α–1 β–2 γ2 in terms of the new units.

Given

  • 1 cal = 4.2 J = 4.2 kg m2 s−2.
  • New units: mass α kg, length β m, time γ s.

Asked

Show that 1 cal = 4.2 α−1 β−2 γ2 new units.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a calculation (or an explanation) with no physical situation to animate; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. In the new units 1 kg = α−1 (new mass units), 1 m = β−1 (new length units) and 1 s = γ−1 (new time units).
  2. So 1 cal = 4.2 kg m2 s−2 = 4.2 × (α−1)(β−1)2(γ−1)−2 = 4.2 α−1 β−2 γ2 new units.

1 cal = 4.2 α−1 β−2 γ2 in the new units.
