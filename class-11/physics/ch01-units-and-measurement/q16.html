1.16 Explain this common observation clearly : If you look out of the window of a fast moving train, the nearby trees, houses etc. seem to move rapidly in a direction opposite to the train’s motion, but the distant objects (hill tops, the Moon, the stars etc.) seem to be stationary. (In fact, since you are aware that you are moving, these distant objects seem to move with you).

Given

  • A fast-moving train; nearby objects (trees, houses) and distant ones (hill tops, the Moon, stars).

Asked

Why nearby objects seem to rush backwards while distant ones seem still.

How the animation is drawn

  • Seen from above; distances and the train’s speed are illustrative.

Answer

  1. What we see as motion is the change in the direction of the line of sight to an object.
  2. For a nearby tree the line of sight turns through a large angle as the train moves a short distance: the tree seems to rush backwards.
  3. For a distant hill the same movement turns the line of sight through a tiny angle (angle ≈ distance moved / distance of the object), so it seems almost still; because we know we are moving, it then seems to move along with us.

The apparent angular speed of an object falls as its distance grows; distant objects barely change direction.
