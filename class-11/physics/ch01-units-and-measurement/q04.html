1.4 Explain this statement clearly : “To call a dimensional quantity ‘large’ or ‘small’ is meaningless without specifying a standard for comparison”. In view of this, reframe the following statements wherever necessary : (a) atoms are very small objects (b) a jet plane moves with great speed (c) the mass of Jupiter is very large (d) the air inside this room contains a large number of molecules (e) a proton is much more massive than an electron (f) the speed of sound is much smaller than the speed of light.

Given

  • Six statements using “small”, “large” or “great”.

Asked

Explain the statement and reframe each sentence where needed.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a calculation (or an explanation) with no physical situation to animate; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. A dimensional quantity is large or small only compared with another quantity of the same kind; a number alone, without a standard, says nothing.
  2. (a) Atoms are very small compared with a sharp pin point (or any object we see).
  3. (b) A jet plane moves faster than a car (or a train).
  4. (c) The mass of Jupiter is very large compared with the mass of the Earth.
  5. (d) The air in this room has more molecules than one mole of air (or than the people in it).
  6. (e) Already a comparison: correct as it stands (a proton is about 1836 times as massive).
  7. (f) Already a comparison: correct as it stands.

Reframe (a)–(d) with a standard of comparison; (e) and (f) already compare two quantities.
