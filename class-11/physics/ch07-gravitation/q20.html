7.20 Two stars each of one solar mass (= 2×1030 kg) are approaching each other for a head on collision. When they are a distance 109 km, their speeds are negligible. What is the speed with which they collide ? The radius of each star is 104 km. Assume the stars to remain undistorted until they collide. (Use the known value of G).

Given

  • Each star 2 × 1030 kg, radius 104 km; start 109 km apart at rest; G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m2 kg−2.

Asked

The speed of each star at collision.

How the animation is drawn

  • Not to scale: the stars are drawn much larger than they are; the fall is shown with time compressed.

Answer

  1. At collision the centres are 2 × 104 km = 2 × 107 m apart.
  2. Energy: −GM2/d₀ = 2 × ½Mv2 − GM2/d, so v2 = GM(1/d − 1/d₀).
  3. v2 = 6.67 × 10−11 × 2 × 1030 × (1/(2 × 107) − 1/1012) ≈ 6.67 × 1012, v ≈ 2.6 × 106 m s−1.

About 2.6 × 106 m s−1 each.
