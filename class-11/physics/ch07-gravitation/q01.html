7.1 Answer the following : (a) You can shield a charge from electrical forces by putting it inside a hollow conductor. Can you shield a body from the gravitational influence of nearby matter by putting it inside a hollow sphere or by some other means ? (b) An astronaut inside a small space ship orbiting around the earth cannot detect gravity. If the space station orbiting around the earth has a large size, can he hope to detect gravity ? (c) If you compare the gravitational force on the earth due to the sun to that due to the moon, you would find that the Sun’s pull is greater than the moon’s pull. (you can check this yourself using the data available in the succeeding exercises). However, the tidal effect of the moon’s pull is greater than the tidal effect of sun. Why ?

Given

  • Three situations about gravity.

Asked

Explanations.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for reasoning or a choice rather than describing one physical situation to follow; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. (a) No. Gravity cannot be screened: matter outside a hollow sphere still attracts a body inside it, and no material blocks gravity.
  2. (b) Yes, in principle: in a large station the gravitational field differs noticeably from one end to the other, and these differences (tidal effects) can be detected.
  3. (c) Tides depend on how much the pull changes across the Earth’s diameter, which varies as M/r3. The Moon is so much closer that its M/r3 is larger than the Sun’s.

(a) No (b) Yes, through variation of g across it (c) tidal effect ∝ M/r3; the Moon is much closer.
