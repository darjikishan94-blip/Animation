6.1 Give the location of the centre of mass of a (i) sphere, (ii) cylinder, (iii) ring, and (iv) cube, each of uniform mass density. Does the centre of mass of a body necessarily lie inside the body ?

Given

  • Uniform sphere, cylinder, ring and cube.

Asked

Where each centre of mass is; whether it must lie inside the body.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for a proof, an explanation or a short calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. For a uniform body the centre of mass is at its geometric centre: (i) the centre of the sphere, (ii) the midpoint of the cylinder’s axis, (iii) the centre of the ring, (iv) the point where the cube’s body diagonals meet.
  2. It need not lie inside the body: for a ring (or a hollow sphere, or a bangle) the centre is empty space.

Geometric centre in each case; no, it can lie outside (e.g. a ring).
