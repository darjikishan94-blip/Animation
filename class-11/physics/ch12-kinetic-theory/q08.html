12.8 Three vessels of equal capacity have gases at the same temperature and pressure. The first vessel contains neon (monatomic), the second contains chlorine (diatomic), and the third contains uranium hexafluoride (polyatomic). Do the vessels contain equal number of respective molecules ? Is the root mean square speed of molecules the same in the three cases? If not, in which case is vrms the largest ?

Given

  • Equal volumes, same T and P; neon (M ≈ 20), chlorine (M ≈ 71), UF6 (M ≈ 352).

Asked

Equal numbers of molecules? Same vrms? Which largest?

How the animation is drawn

  • Molecule speeds in the ratio of vrms (slowed down enormously); molecule sizes illustrative.

Answer

  1. Yes: equal volumes at the same T and P hold equal numbers of molecules (Avogadro’s law, N = PV/kT).
  2. No: vrms = √(3kT/m) depends on the molecular mass; the lighter the molecule, the faster.
  3. Neon, the lightest, has the largest vrms.

Equal numbers; vrms differs, largest for neon.
