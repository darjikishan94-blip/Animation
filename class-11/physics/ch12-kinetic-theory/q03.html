12.3 Figure 12.8 shows plot of PV/T versus P for 1.00× 10–3 kg of oxygen gas at two different temperatures. (a) What does the dotted plot signify? (b) Which is true: T1 > T2 or T1 < T2? (c) What is the value of PV/T where the curves meet on the y-axis? (d) If we obtained similar plots for 1.00× 10–3 kg of hydrogen, would we get the same value of PV/T at the point where the curves meet on the y-axis? If not, what mass of hydrogen yields the same value of PV/T (for low pressure high temperature region of the plot) ? (Molecular mass of H2 = 2.02 u, of O2 = 32.0 u, R = 8.31 J mol–1 K–1.)

Given

  • Fig. 12.8: PV/T against P for 1.00 g of O2 at T1 and T2, and a dotted horizontal line.
  • M(O2) = 32.0 u, M(H2) = 2.02 u, R = 8.31 J mol−1 K−1.

Asked

(a)–(d) as in the question.

How the animation is drawn

  • Curve shapes follow Fig. 12.8.

Answer

  1. (a) The dotted line is ideal-gas behaviour: PV/T = nR, the same at every pressure.
  2. (b) T1 > T2: real gases behave more ideally at higher temperature, and the T1 curve is closer to the dotted line.
  3. (c) At P → 0 every gas is ideal: PV/T = nR = (1.00 × 10−3/32.0 × 10−3) × 8.31 ≈ 0.26 J K−1.
  4. (d) No: 1.00 g of hydrogen is more moles (1/2.02). For the same nR we need the same number of moles, 1/32 mol: m = 2.02/32.0 g ≈ 6.3 × 10−5 kg.

(a) ideal gas (b) T1 > T2 (c) ≈ 0.26 J K−1 (d) No; 6.3 × 10−5 kg of H2.
