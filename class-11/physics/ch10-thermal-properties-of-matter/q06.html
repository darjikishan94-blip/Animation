10.6 A steel tape 1m long is correctly calibrated for a temperature of 27.0 °C. The length of a steel rod measured by this tape is found to be 63.0 cm on a hot day when the temperature is 45.0 °C. What is the actual length of the steel rod on that day ? What is the length of the same steel rod on a day when the temperature is 27.0 °C ? Coefficient of linear expansion of steel = 1.20 × 10–5 K–1 .

Given

  • Tape correct at 27.0 °C; reading 63.0 cm at 45.0 °C; αsteel = 1.20 × 10−5 K−1.

Asked

The rod’s true length at 45.0 °C and at 27.0 °C.

How the animation is drawn

  • The expansion of the marks is exaggerated about 1000 times.

Answer

  1. At 45.0 °C each “1 cm” of the tape is really 1 × (1 + αΔT) cm, with ΔT = 18.0 K.
  2. True length = 63.0 × (1 + 1.20 × 10−5 × 18.0) ≈ 63.0136 cm.
  3. At 27.0 °C the rod (also steel) is shorter by the same factor: about 63.0 cm (the tape is correct then).

≈ 63.0136 cm on the hot day; 63.0 cm at 27.0 °C.
