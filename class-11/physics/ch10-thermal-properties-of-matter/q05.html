10.5 Two ideal gas thermometers A and B use oxygen and hydrogen respectively. The following observations are made : Triple-point of water: A 1.250 × 105 Pa, B 0.200 × 105 Pa; Normal melting point of sulphur: A 1.797 × 105 Pa, B 0.287 × 105 Pa. (a) What is the absolute temperature of normal melting point of sulphur as read by thermometers A and B ? (b) What do you think is the reason behind the slight difference in answers of thermometers A and B ? (The thermometers are not faulty). What further procedure is needed in the experiment to reduce the discrepancy between the two readings ?

Given

  • Pressures at the triple point and at the melting point of sulphur for A (oxygen) and B (hydrogen).

Asked

(a) The two readings; (b) why they differ and how to reduce the difference.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a direct calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. (a) T = 273.16 × P/Ptr. A: 273.16 × 1.797/1.250 ≈ 392.7 K. B: 273.16 × 0.287/0.200 ≈ 392.0 K.
  2. (b) Real gases are not exactly ideal, and differ from each other. Repeat at lower and lower pressures and extrapolate to zero pressure, where all gases behave ideally and the readings agree.

(a) A ≈ 392.7 K, B ≈ 392.0 K (b) non-ideal behaviour; extrapolate to low pressure.
