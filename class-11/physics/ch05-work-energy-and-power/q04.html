5.4 The potential energy function for a particle executing linear simple harmonic motion is given by V(x) = kx2/2, where k is the force constant of the oscillator. For k = 0.5 N m-1, the graph of V(x) versus x is shown in Fig. 5.12. Show that a particle of total energy 1 J moving under this potential must ‘turn back’ when it reaches x = ± 2 m.

Given

  • V(x) = kx2/2, k = 0.5 N m−1; total energy E = 1 J.

Asked

Show the particle turns back at x = ±2 m.

How the animation is drawn

  • The mass is not given; the timing of the oscillation is illustrative.

Answer

  1. E = K + V and K ≥ 0, so the particle can only be where V(x) ≤ 1 J.
  2. V(x) = 1 J gives ½ × 0.5 × x2 = 1, x2 = 4, x = ±2 m.
  3. At x = ±2 m, K = 0: the particle stops for an instant, and the restoring force −kx sends it back.

K = 0 at x = ±2 m, so it turns back there.
