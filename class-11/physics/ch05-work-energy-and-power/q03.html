5.3 Given in Fig. 5.11 are examples of some potential energy functions in one dimension. The total energy of the particle is indicated by a cross on the ordinate axis. In each case, specify the regions, if any, in which the particle cannot be found for the given energy. Also, indicate the minimum total energy the particle must have in each case. Think of simple physical contexts for which these potential energy shapes are relevant.

Given

  • Fig. 5.11: four V(x) graphs with the total energy E marked.

Asked

The forbidden regions; the minimum total energy needed; physical contexts.

How the animation is drawn

  • The four graphs follow the shapes in Fig. 5.11; no values are given.

Answer

  1. K = E − V(x) can never be negative, so the particle cannot be where V(x) > E.
  2. (a) Cannot be in x > a. Minimum total energy: 0. (e.g. a ball on a floor facing a ledge of height set by V0.)
  3. (b) V(x) > E everywhere: it cannot be found anywhere. Minimum total energy: V1, the lowest level of the graph.
  4. (c) Cannot be in x < a or x > b. Minimum total energy: −V1. (A particle in a potential well, e.g. a ball in a pit.)
  5. (d) Cannot be in −b/2 < x < −a/2 or a/2 < x < b/2. Minimum total energy: −V1. (A particle trapped in a well with walls, as in a nucleus.)

(a) x > a; 0 (b) everywhere; V1 (c) x < a and x > b; −V1 (d) −b/2 < x < −a/2 and a/2 < x < b/2; −V1
