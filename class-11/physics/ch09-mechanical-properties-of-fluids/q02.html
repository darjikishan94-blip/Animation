9.2 Explain why (a) The angle of contact of mercury with glass is obtuse, while that of water with glass is acute. (b) Water on a clean glass surface tends to spread out while mercury on the same surface tends to form drops. (Put differently, water wets glass while mercury does not.) (c) Surface tension of a liquid is independent of the area of the surface (d) Water with detergent disolved in it should have small angles of contact. (e) A drop of liquid under no external forces is always spherical in shape

Given

  • Five observations about surfaces of liquids.

Asked

Explanations.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for explanations rather than describing one physical situation to follow; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. (a) For mercury the cohesive forces (Hg–Hg) are stronger than the adhesive forces (Hg–glass): obtuse angle. For water, adhesion to glass beats cohesion: acute angle.
  2. (b) Same reason: water is attracted more to glass than to itself and spreads; mercury pulls itself together into drops.
  3. (c) Surface tension is a force per unit length (or energy per unit area) of the surface: a property of the liquid and its surroundings, not of how much surface there is.
  4. (d) Detergent lowers the surface tension of water against air (and against greasy surfaces), so the water spreads and wets: a small angle of contact. That is what lets it get into the fibres of cloth.
  5. (e) With no other forces, surface tension makes the surface as small as possible; for a given volume the sphere has the least area.

Cohesion vs adhesion; surface tension is a per-length property; detergents reduce it; a sphere has the least area.
