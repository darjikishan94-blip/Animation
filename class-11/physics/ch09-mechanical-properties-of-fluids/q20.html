9.20 What is the excess pressure inside a bubble of soap solution of radius 5.00 mm, given that the surface tension of soap solution at the temperature (20 °C) is 2.50 × 10–2 N m–1 ? If an air bubble of the same dimension were formed at depth of 40.0 cm inside a container containing the soap solution (of relative density 1.20), what would be the pressure inside the bubble ? (1 atmospheric pressure is 1.01 × 105 Pa).

Given

  • r = 5.00 mm; S = 2.50 × 10−2 N m−1; depth 40.0 cm; relative density 1.20; Patm = 1.01 × 105 Pa.

Asked

Excess pressure in the soap bubble; pressure inside the air bubble.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a direct calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. Soap bubble (two surfaces): excess = 4S/r = 4 × 0.025/0.005 = 20.0 Pa.
  2. Air bubble in liquid (one surface): excess = 2S/r = 10.0 Pa.
  3. Pressure in the liquid at 40.0 cm: 1.01 × 105 + 1200 × 9.8 × 0.400 ≈ 1.057 × 105 Pa; inside the bubble ≈ 1.057 × 105 + 10 ≈ 1.06 × 105 Pa.

20.0 Pa; about 1.06 × 105 Pa.
