Exercise 2.1 · When is a body a point object?

2.1 In which of the following examples of motion, can the body be considered approximately a point object: (a) a railway carriage moving without jerks between two stations. (b) a monkey sitting on top of a man cycling smoothly on a circular track. (c) a spinning cricket ball that turns sharply on hitting the ground. (d) a tumbling beaker that has slipped off the edge of a table.

Given

  • Four moving bodies.

Asked

Which can be treated approximately as point objects.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for an explanation (or a judgement) rather than describing one physical situation; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. A body can be treated as a point when its size is small compared with the distance it moves, and its rotation does not matter.
  2. (a) Yes: the carriage is small compared with the distance between stations.
  3. (b) Yes: the monkey is small compared with the size of the circular track.
  4. (c) No: the spin matters, and the turn happens over a distance comparable to the ball’s size.
  5. (d) No: the beaker tumbles and falls a height comparable to its own size.

(a) and (b).
