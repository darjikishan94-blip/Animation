2.12 Look at the graphs (a) to (d) (Fig. 2.10) carefully and state, with reasons, which of these cannot possibly represent one-dimensional motion of a particle.

Given

  • Fig. 2.10: (a) an x–t curve that loops back in time; (b) a v–t circle; (c) a speed–time graph dipping below zero; (d) a total-path-length graph that rises and falls.

Asked

Which graphs are impossible, and why.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for an explanation (or a judgement) rather than describing one physical situation; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. (a) Impossible: at one instant the particle would have two (even three) positions.
  2. (b) Impossible: at one instant it would have two velocities, and the curve also goes back in time.
  3. (c) Impossible: speed can never be negative.
  4. (d) Impossible: total path length can never decrease.

None of the four can represent one-dimensional motion.
