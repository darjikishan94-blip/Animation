2.11 In Exercises 2.9 and 2.10, we have carefully distinguished between average speed and magnitude of average velocity. No such distinction is necessary when we consider instantaneous speed and magnitude of velocity. The instantaneous speed is always equal to the magnitude of instantaneous velocity. Why?

Given

  • Instantaneous speed and instantaneous velocity.

Asked

Why they are always equal in magnitude.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for an explanation (or a judgement) rather than describing one physical situation; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. Instantaneous values are limits over vanishingly small time intervals.
  2. In such a tiny interval the particle cannot turn back, so the path length equals the magnitude of the displacement.
  3. Dividing both by the same tiny time, speed = |velocity|.

Over an infinitesimal interval path length = |displacement|.
