2.10 A man walks on a straight road from his home to a market 2.5 km away with a speed of 5 km h–1. Finding the market closed, he instantly turns and walks back home with a speed of 7.5 km h–1. What is the (a) magnitude of average velocity, and (b) average speed of the man over the interval of time (i) 0 to 30 min, (ii) 0 to 50 min, (iii) 0 to 40 min ? [Note: You will appreciate from this exercise why it is better to define average speed as total path length divided by time, and not as magnitude of average velocity. You would not like to tell the tired man on his return home that his average speed was zero !]

Given

  • Home to market 2.5 km at 5 km h−1; back at 7.5 km h−1, turning instantly.
  • Intervals 0–30 min, 0–50 min, 0–40 min.

Asked

The magnitude of average velocity and the average speed over each interval.

How the animation is drawn

  • 50 minutes shown in 15 s.

Answer

  1. Outward: 2.5/5 = 0.5 h = 30 min. Back: 2.5/7.5 h = 20 min, so home at 50 min.
  2. (i) 0–30 min: displacement = path = 2.5 km in 0.5 h: |average velocity| = average speed = 5 km h−1.
  3. (ii) 0–50 min: displacement 0, path 5 km in 5/6 h: |average velocity| = 0; average speed = 6 km h−1.
  4. (iii) 0–40 min: in the last 10 min he walks back 7.5 × 1/6 = 1.25 km, so displacement 1.25 km, path 3.75 km, in 2/3 h: |average velocity| = 1.875 km h−1; average speed = 5.625 km h−1.

(i) 5, 5 (ii) 0, 6 (iii) 1.875, 5.625 km h−1
