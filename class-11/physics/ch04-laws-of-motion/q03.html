4.3 Give the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on a stone of mass 0.1 kg, (a) just after it is dropped from the window of a stationary train, (b) just after it is dropped from the window of a train running at a constant velocity of 36 km/h, (c) just after it is dropped from the window of a train accelerating with 1 m s−2, (d) lying on the floor of a train which is accelerating with 1 m s−2, the stone being at rest relative to the train. Neglect air resistance throughout.

Given

  • Mass of the stone = 0.1 kg; g = 10 m s−2; air resistance neglected.
  • Train: at rest; at 36 km/h; accelerating at 1 m s−2.

Asked

The magnitude and direction of the net force on the stone in each case.

How the animation is drawn

  • Window height and distances are illustrative; the fall is shown 3 times slower.

Answer

  1. (a) 1 N, vertically downward (only its weight acts).
  2. (b) 1 N, vertically downward: no horizontal force acts once it is released.
  3. (c) 1 N, vertically downward: the train’s acceleration no longer acts on it after release.
  4. (d) 0.1 N (= 0.1 × 1), horizontal, in the direction of the train’s acceleration; weight and normal force cancel.

(a), (b), (c): 1 N downward. (d): 0.1 N horizontal, along the train’s acceleration.
