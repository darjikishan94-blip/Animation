9.29 A Cassegrain telescope uses two mirrors as shown in Fig. 9.26. Such a telescope is built with the mirrors 20 mm apart. If the radius of curvature of the large mirror is 220 mm and the small mirror is 140 mm, where will the final image of an object at infinity be?

Given

  • Primary (concave) R = 220 mm, f = 110 mm; secondary (convex) R = 140 mm, f = 70 mm; 20 mm apart.

Asked

The final image position.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a direct calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. The primary would bring parallel light to a focus 110 mm in front of it, i.e. 110 − 20 = 90 mm behind the secondary: a virtual object for the convex mirror.
  2. For the secondary (taking distances behind it as positive): 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/70 − 1/90 = 2/630, v = 315 mm.
  3. The final image is 315 mm from the secondary mirror, on the side of the primary (295 mm behind the primary, through its central hole).

315 mm from the small mirror, behind the large one.
