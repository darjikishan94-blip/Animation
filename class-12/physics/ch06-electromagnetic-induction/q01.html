6.1 Predict the direction of induced current in the situations described by the following Figs. 6.15(a) to (f).

Given

  • Fig. 6.15 (a)–(f): (a) the S end of a magnet approaching a coil pq; (b) a magnet moving left between two coils; (c) a tapping key just closed in one loop, near a second loop xyz; (d) a rheostat setting being changed; (e) a tapping key just released; (f) a straight wire through a loop, its current decreasing at a steady rate.

Asked

The direction of the induced current in each.

Why there is no animation

  • This question has six separate sketches whose answers depend on the winding sense drawn in Fig. 6.15; the page gives the reasoning and the answers from the NCERT key.

Answer

  1. Lenz’s law: the induced current flows so as to oppose the change in flux that causes it.
  2. (a) The near end of the coil becomes an S pole to repel the approaching S pole: current along qrpq.
  3. (b) The left coil repels the approaching S end and the right coil attracts the receding N end: currents along prq and yzx.
  4. (c) The growing current in the first loop increases the flux through the second; the induced current flows in the opposite sense: along yzx.
  5. (d) Along zyx (NCERT key): the induced current opposes the change of current set by the rheostat.
  6. (e) The current in the first coil is falling; the induced current tries to keep the flux up, in the same sense: along xry.
  7. (f) No induced current: the field lines of the straight wire are circles lying in the plane of the loop, so no flux passes through it.

(a) qrpq (b) prq, yzx (c) yzx (d) zyx (e) xry (f) none.
