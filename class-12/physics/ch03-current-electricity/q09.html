3.9 The number density of free electrons in a copper conductor estimated in Example 3.1 is 8.5 × 1028 m–3. How long does an electron take to drift from one end of a wire 3.0 m long to its other end? The area of cross-section of the wire is 2.0 × 10–6 m2 and it is carrying a current of 3.0 A.

Given

  • n = 8.5 × 1028 m−3; l = 3.0 m; A = 2.0 × 10−6 m2; I = 3.0 A; e = 1.6 × 10−19 C.

Asked

The time to drift 3.0 m.

How the animation is drawn

  • Random motion and time are not to scale; the wire is shortened.

Answer

  1. I = neAvd, so vd = 3.0/(8.5 × 1028 × 1.6 × 10−19 × 2.0 × 10−6) ≈ 1.1 × 10−4 m/s.
  2. t = l/vd = 3.0/1.1 × 10−4 ≈ 2.7 × 104 s (about 7.5 h).

About 2.7 × 104 s (≈ 7.5 h).
