3.3 At room temperature (27.0 °C) the resistance of a heating element is 100 Ω. What is the temperature of the element if the resistance is found to be 117 Ω, given that the temperature coefficient of the material of the resistor is 1.70 × 10–4 °C–1.

Given

  • R₁ = 100 Ω at 27.0 °C; R₂ = 117 Ω; α = 1.70 × 10−4 °C−1.

Asked

The temperature T₂.

Why there is no animation

  • This is a direct calculation with nothing moving to show; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. R₂ = R₁[1 + α(T₂ − T₁)], so T₂ − T₁ = (R₂ − R₁)/(R₁α) = 17/(100 × 1.70 × 10−4) = 1000 °C.
  2. T₂ = 27 + 1000 = 1027 °C.

1027 °C.
