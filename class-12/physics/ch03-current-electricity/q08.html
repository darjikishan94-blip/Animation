3.8 A storage battery of emf 8.0 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω is being charged by a 120 V dc supply using a series resistor of 15.5 Ω. What is the terminal voltage of the battery during charging? What is the purpose of having a series resistor in the charging circuit?

Given

  • Battery: ε = 8.0 V, r = 0.5 Ω. Supply 120 V dc; series resistor 15.5 Ω.

Asked

The terminal voltage while charging; the purpose of the resistor.

How the animation is drawn

  • Circuit schematic; the battery is connected + to +, so the current goes into it at its + terminal.

Answer

  1. The net emf is 120 − 8 = 112 V; the total resistance is 15.5 + 0.5 = 16 Ω: I = 112/16 = 7 A.
  2. While charging, the current enters the + terminal: V = ε + Ir = 8 + 7 × 0.5 = 11.5 V.
  3. The series resistor limits the charging current; without it the current would be (120 − 8)/0.5 = 224 A.

11.5 V; the resistor limits the charging current.
