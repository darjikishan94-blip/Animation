12.8 A 12.5 eV electron beam is used to bombard gaseous hydrogen at room temperature. What series of wavelengths will be emitted?

Given

  • Electron energy 12.5 eV; hydrogen at room temperature (in n = 1); En = −13.6/n2 eV.

Asked

The emitted wavelengths.

How the animation is drawn

  • Energy levels to scale (25 px per eV).

Answer

  1. Excitation energies: to n = 2, 10.2 eV; to n = 3, 12.09 eV; to n = 4, 12.75 eV. 12.5 eV reaches n = 3 at most.
  2. From n = 3 the atom can return as 3 → 1, or 3 → 2 → 1.
  3. λ(3 → 1) = 1240/12.09 ≈ 102.6 nm and λ(2 → 1) = 1240/10.2 ≈ 121.6 nm (Lyman series, ultraviolet); λ(3 → 2) = 1240/1.89 ≈ 656 nm (Balmer series, red).

102.6 nm and 121.6 nm (Lyman); 656 nm (Balmer).
