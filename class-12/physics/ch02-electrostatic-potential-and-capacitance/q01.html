2.1 Two charges 5 × 10–8 C and –3 × 10–8 C are located 16 cm apart. At what point(s) on the line joining the two charges is the electric potential zero? Take the potential at infinity to be zero.

Given

  • q₁ = 5 × 10−8 C, q₂ = −3 × 10−8 C, 16 cm apart; V(∞) = 0. (k = 1/4πε0 = 9 × 109 N m2 C−2; ε0 = 8.854 × 10−12 C2 N−1 m−2.)

Asked

Points on the line where V = 0.

How the animation is drawn

  • The graph is computed from V = kq₁/r₁ + kq₂/r₂ and clipped at ±2 kV.

Answer

  1. Take q₁ at x = 0 and q₂ at x = 16 cm. V = 0 needs 5/r₁ = 3/r₂.
  2. Between them: 5/x = 3/(16 − x), so 80 − 5x = 3x, x = 10 cm.
  3. Beyond q₂: 5/x = 3/(x − 16), so 5x − 80 = 3x, x = 40 cm.
  4. On the far side of q₁ the larger charge is always nearer, so V never reaches zero there.

10 cm and 40 cm from the positive charge, on the side of the negative charge.
