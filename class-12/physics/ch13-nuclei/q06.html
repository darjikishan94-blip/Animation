13.6 Suppose, we think of fission of a 5626Fe nucleus into two equal fragments, 2813Al. Is the fission energetically possible? Argue by working out Q of the process. Given m(5626Fe) = 55.93494 u and m(2813Al) = 27.98191 u.

Given

  • m(Fe) = 55.93494 u; m(Al) = 27.98191 u; 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c2.

Asked

Q; is the fission possible?

How the animation is drawn

  • The mass difference is exaggerated on the balance; numbers exact.

Answer

  1. Q = [m(Fe) − 2m(Al)]c2 = (55.93494 − 55.96382) × 931.5 = −0.02888 × 931.5 ≈ −26.9 MeV.
  2. Q is negative: the products are heavier than iron. The fission cannot happen on its own; energy would have to be supplied.

Q ≈ −26.9 MeV: not energetically possible.
