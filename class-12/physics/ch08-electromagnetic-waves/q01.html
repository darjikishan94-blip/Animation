8.1 Figure 8.5 shows a capacitor made of two circular plates each of radius 12 cm, and separated by 5.0 cm. The capacitor is being charged by an external source (not shown in the figure). The charging current is constant and equal to 0.15A. (a) Calculate the capacitance and the rate of change of potential difference between the plates. (b) Obtain the displacement current across the plates. (c) Is Kirchhoff’s first rule (junction rule) valid at each plate of the capacitor? Explain.

Given

  • Circular plates, r = 0.12 m; d = 0.050 m; air; charging current I = 0.15 A (constant); ε0 = 8.85 × 10−12 C2 N−1 m−2.

Asked

(a) C and dV/dt (b) the displacement current (c) does the junction rule hold at each plate?

How the animation is drawn

  • Side view, 1 px per mm; field lines between the plates thicken as the field grows.

Answer

  1. (a) C = ε0πr2/d = 8.85 × 10−12 × π × 0.122/0.050 ≈ 8.0 × 10−12 F (8.0 pF).
  2. dV/dt = I/C = 0.15/8.0 × 10−12 ≈ 1.9 × 1010 V s−1.
  3. (b) id = ε0 dΦE/dt = C dV/dt = 0.15 A, the same as the conduction current.
  4. (c) Yes, if the displacement current is counted: the conduction current reaching a plate equals the displacement current leaving it across the gap.

(a) 8.0 pF; 1.9 × 1010 V/s (b) 0.15 A (c) Yes, counting the displacement current.
