8.9 The terminology of different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum is given in the text. Use the formula E = hν (for energy of a quantum of radiation: photon) and obtain the photon energy in units of eV for different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum. In what way are the different scales of photon energies that you obtain related to the sources of electromagnetic radiation?

Given

  • E = hν = hc/λ; hc ≈ 1240 eV nm.

Asked

Photon energies for each part of the spectrum; link to the sources.

Why there is no animation

  • This question asks for a short explanation or a table rather than describing one situation to follow; the page shows the question and a worked answer.

Answer

  1. Using E (eV) ≈ 1240/λ (nm):
  2. Radio (λ ≳ 0.1 m): ≲ 10−5 eV. Microwaves (1 mm–0.1 m): 10−5–10−3 eV. Infrared (700 nm–1 mm): 10−3–1.8 eV.
  3. Visible (400–700 nm): 1.8–3.1 eV. Ultraviolet (1–400 nm): 3.1 eV–1.2 keV. X-rays (about 0.001–10 nm): 0.1 keV–1 MeV. Gamma rays (≲ 0.01 nm): ≳ 0.1 MeV.
  4. The photon energy matches the spacing of the energy levels of the source: radio and microwaves from accelerated charges in circuits and molecular rotations; infrared from molecular vibrations; visible and UV from outer (valence) electrons of atoms; X-rays from inner electrons; gamma rays from nuclei.

Energies range from ≲ 10−5 eV (radio) to ≳ 105 eV (γ); each matches the energy-level spacing of its source.
